# ORIGIN (secret: players never read this directly)

The Meridian Lodge opened in March 1971 on a ridge road nobody remembers driving. It looked like a
motor hotel, but it was built and run by the Aster-Vale Foundation, a private institute that studied
"continuous habitation": whether people could live, work and rest without night.

The Foundation's founder, Dr. Ilse Varga, believed sleep was a habit learned from darkness, and
that a building with no night in it would teach its occupants to stop needing one. Every fixture in
the Lodge was wired to a single sealed circuit with no switches. Guests were paid volunteers who
signed on for "a restful week." Staff wore wristwatches with the hands removed.

It worked, for a while. Guests slept less, then not at all, and reported feeling fine. Then the
building started to agree with them. Rooms appeared that were not on the blueprints. Corridors
measured longer on the way back. The night architect, Desmond Hale, kept redrawing the floor plan
to keep up, and in his last memo he wrote that he was no longer sure he was drawing what was there,
or whether the Lodge was building what he drew.

Guests who stopped sleeping completely began to "check out": they walked into rooms that hadn't
existed the day before and did not walk out. The front desk kept their keys. Varga ordered the
staff to keep every light burning, on the theory that whatever was adding rooms could only do it
in the dark, and anything that went dark became part of the new floor plan.

In 1979 the Foundation sealed the Lodge and tried to cut the power. The breakers were thrown. The
meter kept turning. The lights are still on. The building is still growing, one room for every
place that ever went dark inside it, and the staff who stayed to keep the lights burning never
stopped doing their jobs.
